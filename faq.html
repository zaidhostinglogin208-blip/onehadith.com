Frequently Asked Questions

New to Hadith? These answers explain the basics, how Hadith are evaluated, and how to use One Hadith as a learning resource. References are included so you can read further.

01 What is Hadith?+

A Hadith is a transmitted report about something the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said, did, or approved. Hadith reports help Muslims learn about the Prophet’s guidance and practice.

A report commonly has two main parts: the isnad (the chain of narrators) and the matn (the report’s text). Hadith scholars examine both.

Further reading: Introduction to Hadith.

02 What is the difference between the Quran and Hadith?+

The Quran is the revealed Book of Allah and is recited as Quran in worship. Hadith are transmitted reports about the Prophet’s words, actions, and approvals. They are not the same type of text and a Hadith is not part of the Quran.

The Quran also describes the Prophet’s role in explaining the revelation (Surah An-Nahl 16:44). Muslims use the Quran and the established Sunnah together, while checking the authenticity and meaning of individual reports.

References: Quran 16:44 · Quran and Hadith: the difference.

03 Are all Hadith authentic?+

No. Hadith reports have different levels of reliability. Some are graded Sahih (sound), some Hasan (good), and some Da'if (weak). Reports may also be judged fabricated (Mawdu').

A report should not be treated as authentic just because it is popular, appears in a social-media post, or is shared with a reference. Its wording, source, chain and scholarly grading matter.

Further reading: How scholars distinguish sound and weak Hadith.

04 What are Sahih, Hasan, and Da'if Hadith?+

Sahih: a sound report that meets the established conditions of authenticity, including a connected chain and reliable, precise narrators, without a stronger conflicting report or a hidden defect.

Hasan: a good/acceptable report. Its narrators meet the relevant reliability conditions, though the precision of one or more narrators is generally below the level required for Sahih.

Da'if: a weak report that does not meet one or more conditions required for acceptance. The reason and degree of weakness can vary. These are brief introductory definitions; Hadith grading has further technical detail.

Further reading: Guidelines for grading Hadith.

05 How can I verify a Hadith?+

Start by finding the exact wording, the original collection and reference number. Then check the chain of narrators (isnad), the text (matn), other versions of the report, and the grading given by qualified Hadith scholars.

Translations and numbering can differ between editions and websites. A search result or a website label alone is not a substitute for scholarly evaluation. If the grading is unclear or scholars differ, ask a qualified teacher or scholar rather than deciding from a short quote.

Further reading: The stages of Hadith verification.

06 Which Hadith books are considered authentic?+

Among the most well-known Sunni Hadith collections are the six books commonly called al-Kutub al-Sittah: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami' al-Tirmidhi, Sunan al-Nasa'i, and Sunan Ibn Majah.

Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim are widely regarded by the majority of Sunni scholars as the most authentic Hadith collections. This does not mean that every report in every other collection has the same grade; individual narrations still need to be assessed.

Further reading: About Sahih al-Bukhari · About Sahih Muslim · The Six Books.

07 Why do Hadith have different grades?+

Hadith are graded because reports differ in their chains, narrators and supporting evidence. Scholars study whether narrators were trustworthy and accurate, whether the chain is connected, and whether the report has contradictions or hidden defects.

Different routes of transmission and the evidence available to a scholar can sometimes lead to different scholarly assessments. A grade is a result of specialist evaluation, not a rating of how inspiring or popular a saying sounds.

Further reading: How Hadith are evaluated.

08 Can I share Hadith on social media?+

Yes, but share responsibly. Check the exact wording, source, reference and grading first. Keep the context with the quote and do not attribute a saying to the Prophet ﷺ when you cannot verify that attribution.

If you are unsure about its authenticity or meaning, pause and ask someone qualified instead of forwarding it as fact. A reference number is helpful, but it should lead to the actual report and its scholarly assessment.

Further reading: Guidelines for checking a Hadith.

09 Do I need to follow every Hadith?+

Muslims are called to follow the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ, but applying an individual Hadith requires understanding its authenticity, wording, context and intended ruling. Not every authentic report is phrased as an obligation; some describe recommended actions, permissions, events or particular circumstances.

Scholars consider the Quran, other relevant Hadith, the Arabic wording and established principles of Islamic jurisprudence when explaining how a report applies. For a personal religious ruling, consult a qualified scholar rather than relying on an isolated translation.

Quran reference: Surah An-Nahl 16:44.

10 How can One Hadith help me learn Islam?+

One Hadith is designed to make reading and reflecting on Hadith simple. You can read one Hadith at a time, view its available source details and references, and use the English and Bangla text and learning sections to help understand its message.

When signed in, you can also save Hadith to your Favorite List or Read Later list. The explanations and real-life examples on the website are educational aids; they are not a replacement for the original source, a qualified teacher, or a scholar’s guidance.

Important note: These FAQs are introductory educational information, not personal fatwas. Hadith wording, numbering and grading can vary across editions and scholarly assessments. Always return to the source and consult qualified scholars for detailed religious questions.
